# Rotating snakes: strengthening review

25 October 2026. The owner reports that OpticEye's drawing “pales in comparison” to [Bach's example](https://michaelbach.de/ot/mot-snakes/). After reviewing the first curved seven-ring prototype, the owner said the right-hand proposal was better but still much weaker than Bach's example, and supplied a reference screenshot. No device, browser, zoom, DPR or distance was supplied for either observation. These are viewing responses, not successful calibration or listing approval.

## What differs

Bach's rendered example, the supplied screenshot and OpticEye's published page were visually inspected. Bach shows thirteen tightly packed wheels with numerous concentric rings and rounded **blue/yellow** elements over black/white regions, against a white surrounding field. The first review incorrectly described the rounded elements as black/white and built its prototype that way; the four-color centerline sequence alone did not capture the reference's two-dimensional boundaries. OpticEye's deployed version has four separated wheels, only three coarse annuli, straight sector boundaries and a gray field. The reference's flat blue/yellow colors were sampled in the browser; no reference artwork is bundled or traced.

| Ingredient | Current OpticEye | Inspected Bach example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Element shape | Four equal annular wedges per cycle | Rounded blue/yellow elements between black/white regions |
| Rings | Three, separated by gaps | Many, tightly nested |
| Blue / yellow | `#0000FF` / `#FFFF00` | `#0066FF` / `#D2D200` |
| Surround | `#808080` | White |

The standard sRGB transfer function and relative-luminance weights give approximate Y values of **0.072/0.928** for our blue/yellow and **0.167/0.598** for the sampled pair, relative to white=1. These are calculated sRGB values, not measurements of either viewer's display. The difference makes palette selection worth testing; it does not establish the cause of the owner's response.

## Evidence and limits

- **Kitaoka & Ashida (2003), [Phenomenal characteristics of the peripheral drift illusion](https://www.psy.ritsumei.ac.jp/~akitaoka/PDrift.pdf)**: the full two-page paper was read. It reports stronger peripheral drift with curved or fragmented edges than with long edges, and emphasizes the ordered luminance steps. This directly motivates curved units. It does not imply that every straight-sector pattern fails.
- **Atala-Gérard & Bach (2017), [Rotating Snakes Illusion—Quantitative Analysis Reveals a Region in Luminance Space With Opposite Illusory Rotation](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2041669517691779)**: full text read. The intermediate luminances influence strength and direction; its calibrated laboratory values cannot simply be treated as browser RGB settings.
- **[Bach's luminance-control demonstration](https://michaelbach.de/ot/mot-snakesLum/)**: explanatory text read. It deliberately simplifies the original to wedges and discusses luminance-dependent variation. Neighboring reversed sequences can make relative motion more conspicuous. Keep our alternating neighboring directions; do not promise a particular perceived direction or that a symmetric pattern eliminates every sensation.

More rings and a white surface are construction hypotheses based on the inspected example, not independently established improvements. No claim is made that the proposed variation reproduces Kitaoka's or Bach's measured strength. Source-site artwork has its own restrictions; attribution alone does not authorize copying it. The proposed SVG uses independently specified geometric primitives, not an imported, traced or modified source image.

## Concrete proposal

[Specification section 6](specs/rotating-snakes.md#6-proposed-curved-element-revision--awaiting-owner-approval) retains the first, unapproved proposal as the comparison baseline. Its black/white ellipses on blue/yellow sectors were a specific design mismatch. The latest [prototype review in section 7](specs/rotating-snakes.md#7-further-prototype-review--not-implementation-approval) tests independently constructed blue/yellow ellipses over black/white sectors, an original fourteen-wheel staggered field, eighteen rings extending near the center and fixed element tilt. Swapping the rounded color roles preserves the black → blue → white → yellow centerline sequence while changing which boundaries are curved. It is a hypothesis to test, not a proven strength improvement.

The standalone comparison outside Git now keeps the first proposal as its left-hand baseline and allows separate changes to rounded color roles, layout, ring count, radius ratio, fixed tilt and ring phase. It also offers a full-width800px view so the new drawing is not always squeezed into one comparison column. “Only correct rounded colors” isolates the role correction; “Match previous prototype” restores the baseline. These diagnostic selectors and800px setting are not approved production controls/defaults. The fourteen-wheel/eighteen-ring test has12,096 primitives at its fixed12 cycles per ring; subpixel center details are expected. Density, tilt and phase have not been established as individually beneficial. Keep the application controls and final choices undecided until owner review.

The owner found the second prototype better, then requested that the blue and yellow-green ovals face directly inward. The review default is now radial (β0°), replacing the exploratory20° tilt. Each oval's long axis points toward its wheel center, perpendicular to the local ring tangent; a single global90° orientation would not do this around a circle. This is an explicit owner design preference, not evidence of a universal strength improvement. The overall application revision remains unapproved pending review.

The owner subsequently responded positively to the radial orientation and asked to reduce white gaps, clarifying that these meant the gaps between colored ovals within each ring. The latest prototype increases only their tangential width by30% by default; ring radii, centers, radial length, inward orientation and wheel packing stay fixed. Original/1.30/1.50 widths remain available for review. Both white and black intervening regions narrow as the colored ellipses widen; no blending or recoloring of the remaining backgrounds is introduced. No stronger-effect claim follows from this area change.

The owner reported that widening barely changed the intended gap and supplied two close-ups. Pixel matching located them exactly in the prior800px render at `(17,34,30,43)` and `(8,14,55,76)` (x,y,width,height), at the first wheel's outer-left edge. The next diagnostic changes spacing directly: move the two colored ovals bordering each white sector toward one another, retaining their size, radial distance and inward axes. The default leaves25% of their former free angular gap, using the ellipse's exact angular silhouette to avoid overlaps across all selectable widths/tilts. White sectors shrink and black sectors widen; the four-color sequence remains. A same-coordinate magnified before/after view exposes the exact pocket. This gap fraction is an owner-directed trial, not a measured optimum or an application release.

The owner then requested a smaller black gap and a slightly larger white gap, supplying a close-up from Bach's example. The latest default moves g from0.25 to0.40: free white/black gaps become40%/160% of the uniform-spacing gap, previously25%/175%. With the current12-cycle/q0.80/1.30-width geometry, this transfers about0.783° per gap from black to white. Oval size, color and inward orientation stay fixed. The crop is a qualitative viewing reference, not traced geometry or a measured specification for Bach's artwork.

The owner explicitly requested another reduction of the black separation between blue and yellow-green ovals. The current trial default is g0.70, reducing that free angular black gap18.75% relative to g0.40; the colored shapes retain their size and radial axes. Review options preserve earlier settings, and the close-up comparison now shows the black section at g0.40 versus the new default. This remains prototype tuning without a new perceptual-strength or calibration claim.

On the further request to shrink the black gap again, the latest prototype default is g1.30. Free black angular separation becomes70% of uniform spacing, about46% smaller than at g0.70; white separation increases correspondingly. Ovals retain their size, colors and inward orientation. The allowed gap range now crosses uniform spacing, so checks explicitly require positive separation on both black and white sides (`0<g<2`). No application replacement or successful human calibration is inferred.

The owner then reported that white had become too large. Repeatedly changing the balance at fixed count traded one gap against the other. The current trial increases actual cycle count from12 to14 while holding the oval-width reference at12, preserving every oval's radial/tangential dimensions; default g becomes0.85. This reduces total blank spacing instead of reallocating it alone. At the current default geometry, white free angular separation decreases about61.5% and black about3.2% relative to the preceding12-cycle/g1.30 trial. Review can switch12/14 cycles separately; the magnifier compares the prior12-cycle/g1.30 configuration with the new one. Counts/defaults remain a review proposal, not an application release or proven perceptual improvement.
